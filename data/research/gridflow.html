GridFlow: decentralized flow matching

This research proposes a novel approach for decentralised large-scale generative modelling. Among the main approaches, sequential dependencies make autoregressive models difficult to decentralise, while diffusion and flow matching frameworks can be partitioned across both data space and time, which we explore in GridFlow:

We split the data into subsets and the generative trajectory into time segments, forming a grid of small models trained without inter-node communication. At sampling time, a router combines their predictions. The best grids concentrate experts in the early, noisy stages and share a single final block. Clustering, router training and the final block still use the full dataset:

At matched training compute, our best grids outperform a single model on CIFAR-10 but still trail it on ImageNet-256. They reduce per-device memory and sampling costs compared with data-only decentralisation, pointing towards collaborative training on smaller machines with slow connections. See the paper for details.

In the future, we are particularly interested in large-scale generative modelling for robust decentralised safeguards and proactive defences against rapidly evolving, interconnected agentic threats across multiple fronts, such as through international open-source efforts.
